AI assistants are already in most organizations, whether leadership approved them or not. The question isn't whether your people will use them. It's whether they'll use them in a way that protects the people you serve.

## Start with the work, not the tool

Ask your team where their time goes. The best first uses of AI are usually unglamorous: drafting routine replies, summarizing long documents, cleaning up notes. Pick one or two of those and measure whether they actually save time before expanding.

## Set three guardrails before you scale

1. **Know what data goes in.** Decide which information may never be pasted into an AI tool: client records, credentials, anything covered by a confidentiality agreement.
2. **Choose approved tools.** Prefer business plans with clear data-handling terms over personal accounts, and make the approved list easy to find.
3. **Keep a human accountable.** AI drafts; a person reviews and owns the result. That's a leadership principle, not just a security one.

## Review it like any other system

Treat AI like the rest of your stack: an owner, access that matches responsibility, and a review on a schedule. Adoption done this way builds trust instead of eroding it.
